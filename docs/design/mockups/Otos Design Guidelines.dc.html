Otos · Design guidelines · v0.1 draft
A macOS system, calibrated
Otos uses Apple's semantic type styles, label colors, materials and control metrics as-is. Nothing here invents a typeface or a palette. What it does is decide which semantic role each piece of Otos content gets, so that hierarchy is a rule rather than a judgement call per screen. Where Otos deviates from the platform default, the deviation is marked Otos and justified.
01
Principles
Lifted from the repo's own architecture invariants, restated as things you can check a screen against.
Show the outcome, not the machine
The user reads results and consequences. Internal vocabulary — plan, disposition, inventory, replan, companion, execution — never reaches a label, even when it is the most accurate word available.
One fact, one owner
Any given piece of state is rendered in exactly one place at a time. A second rendering is only allowed when the first is off-screen, and then it is smaller and quieter — an indicator, not a report.
Honest numbers, or none
Never show a denominator that can move, or a percentage the app is guessing at. If the total isn't known yet, say what is known and count up. Numerals always, never spelled out; tabular figures wherever a number changes in place.
Reassure rather than warn
Originals are read-only and copies are additive, so almost nothing in Otos is dangerous. Reserve alerts and destructive styling for the few operations that actually destroy something — erasing a card, removing a library.
The content is the interface
Photographs carry all the colour in this app. Chrome stays achromatic and quiet, and the accent colour appears only on the thing you should act on or the thing that is selected — never as decoration.
Predictable before clever
Where the platform has an answer — a popup menu, a sheet, a toolbar, a progress bar — use it in its ordinary form. Familiarity is the feature. Custom behaviour needs a reason that survives being written down.

Two weights, three sizes
Any single surface uses at most regular and semibold, and at most three of the sizes above. Bold, italic and letter-spacing tricks are not part of the system.
Numbers
Anything that ticks gets .monospacedDigit() — counts, byte totals, durations, sidebar item counts. Text must never reflow while a number changes.
No custom faces
SF only, plus SF Mono in developer-facing surfaces (logs, diagnostics). A custom face would break the one thing this app is trading on.

Grouping rules — the CRAP pass
Proximity: a number and the control that acts on it belong within 8pt of each other, or the number belongs inside the control's title. Alignment: field labels in a group share a left edge and a fixed label column width; nothing is centred. Repetition: the same relationship looks the same everywhere — label left, control right, 7pt gap, everywhere in the app. Contrast: adjacent levels must differ in both size and colour; two things at the same level, side by side, must be separated by space or a hairline, not by a size tweak.
Reading order for a status bar
Left is what (L1 over L3), centre is choices behind a hairline, right is the act. That ordering is not decoration: it matches the sentence the user is forming — "42 items, into this library, go" — and it puts the primary button where every macOS sheet and dialog puts it. Zones never swap sides between states, and the bar keeps one fixed height across all states so content above it never reflows.

Accent
System accent, whatever the user has set. Selection, the one prominent button, focus rings, determinate progress. Never a background fill larger than a control, never a decorative tint.
Green
Completion that has been verified, and nothing else. A checkmark on a filled circle, at 22pt, once per completed operation. Not for "on", not for health.
Orange
Needs attention, nothing lost — unreadable files, unavailable library, skipped items. Always paired with a way to look at the thing.
Red
Destruction only: erasing a card, removing a library. Per HIG, a destructive action the user deliberately chose doesn't get red — red is for the one they might have hit by accident.
Contrast floor: 4.5:1 for anything at L1 or L2, 3:1 for L3. Secondary label on the bar background clears 4.5:1 in both appearances; tertiary is reserved for text that is decorative-by-role. Colour is never the only signal — the orange badge carries a glyph, the green carries a checkmark, selection carries a border as well as a tint.
05
Status and copy
Every long-running operation in Otos — scan, enrich, import, reorganize — exposes the same three user-facing states, regardless of how many internal states it has. Two extra states exist only because they're actionable: needs attention and failed.
Preparing
Present participle, no ellipsis stacking: "Looking through the card…". Indeterminate bar, always moving. Count up if you can ("Checked 112 of 181"); say nothing if you can't. Primary action stays visible and disabled — never removed.
Working
Determinate bar, items in the primary line, bytes and remaining time in the supporting line. Cancel present whenever cancelling is safe, labelled Cancel, no confirmation — because nothing is lost.
Done
Past tense, and it states the guarantee: "42 items copied and verified". Where the result went, then a way to go there. Auto-dismisses after ~6s; the result stays reachable in the sidebar.

Sentence rules
One clause per line. At most one middot per line, separating two facts of equal rank — a third fact means a second line or a different level. No em-dash chains. Nouns the user owns: items, photos, videos, card, library. Buttons are verb phrases naming the result and carrying the count: "Import 42 Items", "Eject Card", "Erase Card". Sentence case everywhere except button titles, which take title case per macOS.
Where status may appear
A status bar at the bottom of the content pane owns the operation that belongs to the current selection. A sidebar row may show a small indicator — spinner or 32pt determinate bar — only for an operation whose bar is not currently visible. Toolbar never carries status. Sheets are for a scoped task with a Cancel; read-only inspection is a panel or an inline disclosure, not a modal.

Metrics, controls, grid
A 4pt base unit. Only these steps: 2, 4, 7, 8, 12, 16, 20, 28. Radii: 4 tile, 6 small control, 7 push button, 9–11 window and card.

Push buttons 30pt in a bar, 24pt in dense inspectors; popups and small controls 22pt; checkboxes 14pt with a 12pt label. Minimum click target 28×28 including padding. Exactly one prominent button per surface, and it is the one the Return key fires. A disabled primary keeps its place and its title — controls never appear and disappear between states.
Grid and tiles
0:27
Tile radius 4, 4pt inset per item (8pt gutters), 12pt section inset — as built today. Selection is a 2.5pt accent border on the thumbnail face, not a fill, so the photograph is never tinted. Badges are bottom-right, black 55%, radius 3, and appear only once a real thumbnail has painted. Placeholders use the kind glyph at tertiary strength; failures use the slash variant. No shadows, no hover lift, no rounded-corner drift between zoom levels.
Ideas for the grid, offered rather than assumed
Three things would make the grid read better without changing what it is, and they'd work identically for a library and a card. One: day headers (.headline plus a secondary count) with the header pinning while its run scrolls — Photos' structure, and it gives the eye an anchor in a 1,374-item scroll. Two: square tiles by default with aspect-fit contents on a neutral face, so rows form clean columns; the current aspect-shaped tiles create ragged rows that make scanning harder. Offer the current behaviour as a "vary tile shape" preference if you like it. Three: a two-line hover overlay at the bottom of the tile (filename, capture time) at the two largest zoom levels only, since at small sizes it covers the photo. All three are additive and reversible.
07
Motion and accessibility
Motion
Motion explains a change in position or state; it never announces itself. Three durations: 0.15s ease-out for state within a control, 0.25s ease-out for content appearing, 0.35s spring (response 0.35, damping 0.85) for a surface entering or leaving. The status bar slides up 24pt with a fade on card mount and leaves the same way. State changes cross-fade text in place while the bar's height stays fixed. The completion checkmark scales from 0.6 with a light overshoot — the one flourish in the app. Progress bars animate continuously and are never left stationary. Grid reorders animate positions only, never opacity, and coalesce into one pass. Under Reduce Motion every one of these becomes a 0.15s cross-fade; the checkmark simply appears.
Accessibility
Every operation is reachable and completable with the keyboard alone: Tab reaches the bar's controls in reading order, Return fires the prominent button, Escape cancels. Focus rings are never suppressed. VoiceOver labels lead with what distinguishes the element — filename first on a tile, as the tile presentation layer already does — then kind, then state; a status bar announces its primary line as a live region when it changes state, not on every tick. Under Increase Contrast, hairlines go to the system separator strength and the tertiary level is promoted to secondary. Under Reduce Transparency, bar and sidebar become opaque at the values in §04. Nothing is communicated by colour alone, and no text below 10pt ships.
Open for you: whether square tiles and day headers are welcome; whether "Copy into" or "Destination" is the right label; whether the completion bar should also offer Photos' Keep/Erase choice for the card rather than a pre-flight checkbox. Once these settle, this becomes the reference every screen is reviewed against, and I'll apply it to grid, loupe, compare, sidebar and settings in that order.
